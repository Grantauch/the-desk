# Blitzkrieg review

The adversarial and neutral reviews started with the live release from PR #390. The audit covered the authored TypeScript, TSX and CSS, the static page and build entry, and all 25 images used in the map, combat and effects. The shipped JavaScript and CSS are generated from the source. The vendored React distributions are third-party code.

| Area | Baseline finding | Change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Campaign and economy | The original map was the only layout. A greedy Bofors-only build completed all 12 waves with all 22 garrison and 626 zł. | Three routes with different coverage and time-to-exit. Bofors remains the aircraft counter and deals substantially less ground damage. Map progress is saved separately. |
| Preview and pacing | The first tower recommendation arrived after launch. Empty wave 1 took about 64 simulated seconds. | Wave composition and counter advice now appear before launch, with an advance warning before aircraft arrive. The two new maps have shorter paths, and aircraft take a direct approach. |
| Placement and gun handling | The map resized when a gun was selected. Double-click did not reliably crew it. Hotkeys could select an unaffordable gun. | A fixed-height narrow layout keeps the map in place. Added keyboard placement and aiming, a double-click handler, affordability checks, and compatible armed and crew states. |
| Decisions and combat | Both upgrade tracks could be maxed. Direct fire had an unintended long reach. Ground units could intercept shots aimed at aircraft. | The last rank is an exclusive specialization. Direct projectiles stop at the gun's nominal range. Automated AA shots can reach their air targets through overlapping ground units. |
| Terrain and props | The 1792×1008 terrain image looked disconnected from the opaque road. Tree, pine, hay and sandbag sprites read as props, but they had little tactical visual hierarchy. | Translucent road strokes retain the field texture. The boards have distinct routes and tint, passable tiles reflect the displayed terrain props, and entry, exit and flight paths are marked. |
| Towers, enemies and effects | The four tower sprites and eight enemy silhouettes are different at native scale, but their size makes them easy to confuse on a short viewport. The four projectile sprites and four explosion frames include visible bright fringes. The damage flash was overwritten and the full-screen alert remained under reduced motion. | Visible ground/air routes and an aircraft marker support recognition. Hit flash works, the zoom pans less, and reduced motion disables camera zoom, shake and screen flash. The original image set is retained. |
| Audio | Gunfire repeatedly allocated and filled noise buffers. Overlapping cues could stack and muting allowed playing tails to finish. | One reused buffer, per-cue spacing, a master gain and a limiter. |
| Engine, shell and build | The 60 Hz browser loop and balance probe used different steps. Cooldowns, timers, state transitions, saves and browser input require coordinated tests. | The probe uses the browser timestep. Added regression checks for each route, mixed and single-gun balance, placement, crew mode, upgrades, range and legacy progress. The briefing and result use native modal focus containment. |

The new routes share the original terrain artwork and wave roster. The sprite contact sheet also showed several effects with colored halos and several units that merge visually at phone scale. A subsequent art pass can replace those assets alongside bespoke terrain, and the campaign can give each of the three maps its own wave sequence. Local progress records cleared waves and victories. It does not resume an interrupted run.
