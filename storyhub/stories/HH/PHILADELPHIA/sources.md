# the ship that never vanished

The ship existed. The invisibility and teleportation story lacks supporting documentary evidence.

## S1 · Naval History and Heritage Command, Philadelphia Experiment

https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/p/philadelphia-experiment.html

The Navy summarizes its searches and the ship’s war diary. This is a later institutional account of contemporary records; the lesson distinguishes it from reproducing the original log itself.

## S2 · Office of Naval Research, information sheet (1996)

https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/p/philadelphia-experiment/philadelphia-experiment-onr-info-sheet.html

Traces the story through correspondence after a 1955 book and the annotated copies. Its proposed explanations for the legend are hypotheses about origins, not proof of how every teller learned the story.

## S3 · Royal New Zealand Navy Museum, degaussing

https://navymuseum.co.nz/explore/by-themes/technology-and-weapons/degaussing-2/

Explains the real reduction of a ship’s magnetic signature. Magnetic mine detection, human sight and radar are different questions.

## S4 · Dictionary of American Naval Fighting Ships, Eldridge

https://www.history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/danfs/e/eldridge.html

The official ship history establishes commissioning and actual escort service. An ordinary service summary is useful context, not a complete original daily log.
