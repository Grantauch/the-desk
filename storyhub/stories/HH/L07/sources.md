## Research basis
- [United States Holocaust Memorial Museum — International Military Tribunal](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/international-military-tribunal-at-nuremberg): tribunal, charges, defendants and verdicts.

- Joseph Kingsbury-Smith, contemporary pool report, October 16, 1946, as discussed in the Nagorski historical analysis.
- [TIME, October 28, 1946](https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0,33009,803997-2,00.html): contemporary journalistic repetition of Woods's claimed experience.

## Historical distinctions
- Twelve condemned, ten executed on October 16, 1946. Göring died by suicide before execution; Bormann was tried in absentia and was later established to have died earlier.
- Woods's press claim of 347 executions is *attributed*, not a record-backed actual total.
- MacLean's 60–100 estimate is a historian's research estimate, not a confirmed precise count.
- Kingsbury-Smith's report documents sounds heard. The sound does not by itself establish exact death times or Woods's subjective intention.
- The U.S. Army denied that the executions were mishandled. Contemporary eyewitness claims and later historians' assessments vary.
- Woods died of electrocution in 1950; unsupported claims of murder or work on an electric chair should not be repeated as established.
