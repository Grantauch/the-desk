# HH L06 — Two Sources — public source notes

This StoryHub compares the source structure of the 1969 “Paul is dead” rumor with the Watergate investigation. It studies the rumor's method without reproducing album art or inviting a new clue hunt.

## Primary and period sources

- WKNR Detroit broadcast material from October 12, 1969, and later accounts of the caller and Russ Gibb's on-air response.
- Fred LaBour, “McCartney Dead; New Evidence Brought to Light,” *The Michigan Daily*, October 14, 1969, together with LaBour's later statements that he invented clues as satire.
- *Life* magazine, November 1969, showing Paul McCartney alive.
- *Washington Post* Watergate reporting from 1972 to 1974 and accounts of the paper's independent-source standard under Ben Bradlee.
- Senate Watergate Committee testimony of Alexander Butterfield, July 16, 1973, confirming the White House taping system.
- *United States v. Nixon* (1974) and the public record of President Nixon's August 9, 1974 resignation.

## Institutional references

- National Archives Watergate records and Nixon tapes collection.
- Miller Center presidential recordings and transcript resources.
- Published histories of the rumor's Michigan origins and the Watergate reporting process.

## Boundaries and disagreements

- Accounts differ on the precise national spread sequence. The page uses “about three weeks” and does not fabricate a station-by-station chronology.
- LaBour's intention is reported as his later account. The page says he wrote satire and invented most, not necessarily every, familiar clue.
- The Post's rule is stated as requiring a second independent confirmation for anonymous information; exact newsroom formulations vary by account.
- No single event cleanly ended the rumor. The page says it faded through loss of interest.
- Native HTML receiver, newspaper, and network panels are explanatory graphics. They are not archival images, likenesses, or facsimiles.
