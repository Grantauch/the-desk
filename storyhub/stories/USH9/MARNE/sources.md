# Thirty Miles From Paris: sources and limits

Reviewed 2026-10-28. ByteSize no. 21, US History 9. Front Lines battle 2 of 21.

## History

- Holger H. Herwig, *The Marne, 1914* (Random House, 2009): army strengths, the Hentsch mission, Bülow's "cinders," Kuhl's reaction, the step dates on the sand table.
- Wikipedia, First Battle of the Marne, used for its summary of the casualty estimates (Herwig, Sumner, Nelipovich) and strengths: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Battle_of_the_Marne
- Wikipedia, Renault Taxi de la Marne: about 600 cabs, groups of 350 and 250 leaving Les Invalides at 10 p.m. and an hour later, five soldiers per cab, about 4,000 men of the 62nd Division, 70,012 francs reimbursed, the 6th Army's 150,000 mostly by train: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Taxi_de_la_Marne
- National Army Museum, First Battle of the Marne: https://ww1.nam.ac.uk/timeline/first-battle-marne/
- Air & Space Forces Magazine, "The Influence of Airpower on the Marne," July 2011: Breguet's reconnaissance, RFC reports, Sir John French's dispatch of September 7: https://www.airandspaceforces.com/article/0711marne/
- Gallieni's proclamation of September 3, 1914 and Joffre's order of the day of September 6, 1914: translated for the page from the French texts.
- Moltke's letter of September 9, 1914, in Winston Churchill, *The World Crisis, 1911 to 1918* (Free Press, 2005), p. 168.
- Jean Michel Steg, *Le jour le plus meurtrier de l'histoire de France: 22 août 1914* (Fayard, 2013): about 27,000 French dead on August 22, 1914.

## Choices and limits

- Casualty totals are shown as ranges and labeled as estimates.
- The taxis are presented as a morale story with a marginal military effect, which is the standard historical judgment.
- Foch's "situation excellent, I attack" and Moltke's "we have lost the war" are presented as unproven, since neither appears in a contemporary record.
- The sand table uses real coordinates for rivers and towns. Army blocks mark rough centers only and the page says so.
- The 3D taxi is an interpretive model built from primitives, labeled on the page.

## Images

All photographs come from the Library of Congress Prints and Photographs Division, Bain News Service collection (and one Harris & Ewing portrait of Sir John French). The Library lists no known restrictions on publication for these collections. Item pages are recorded per asset in assets.json. Each image was cropped to remove the caption strip and paper margin, converted to grayscale webp and lightly contrast corrected. None are colorized or generated.

No photograph of Richard Hentsch, Max von Hausen, Hans von Gronau or Louis Franchet d'Espèrey was cleared for this release. They appear by name only.
