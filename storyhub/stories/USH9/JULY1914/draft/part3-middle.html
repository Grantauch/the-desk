Sarajevo · Sunday, June 28, 1914

A wrong turn on Franz Joseph Street

Archduke Franz Ferdinand, next in line to rule Austria Hungary, visited Sarajevo with his wife Sophie to watch army exercises. Bosnia had belonged to Austria Hungary only since 1908, and many Bosnian Serbs wanted it joined to Serbia. June 28 was Vidovdan, a Serbian national holiday marking the Battle of Kosovo in 1389.

Six young conspirators, armed by a secret Serbian officers' society called the Black Hand, waited along the riverside road. At about 10:10 Nedeljko Čabrinović threw a bomb that bounced off the archduke's car and wounded officers in the car behind. After a reception at City Hall, the driver turned onto Franz Joseph Street by mistake and stopped to back up. Gavrilo Princip was standing on that corner. He fired twice, and both the archduke and Sophie were dead before noon.

Count Franz von Harrach, riding on the car's running board, later reported the archduke's last words to his wife.

"Sopherl! Don't die! Stay alive for our children!"Franz Ferdinand, as recorded by Count Harrach

Map 4 · The Western Front, 1914

Six weeks to Paris

Germany sat between France and Russia. Its answer was a plan drawn up by Count Alfred von Schlieffen in 1905. Most of the army would go through neutral Belgium, loop west of Paris and beat France in about six weeks. Then the troops would ride trains east to face Russia, which German planners expected to need about six weeks to get ready.
