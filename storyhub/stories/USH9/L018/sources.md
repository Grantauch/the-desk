## Outside research — exact evidence and gaps

**Historical wheat prices.** Average U.S. farm wheat price: 83.9¢ in 1891, 62.4¢ in 1892, 53.8¢ in 1893, and 49.1¢ in 1894.  
https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AEB1911_-_Volume_01.djvu/456

**Historical cotton prices.** USDA/NASS series including 8.59¢ per pound in 1890 and 4.59¢ in 1894.  
https://www.nass.usda.gov/Publications/Todays_Reports/reports/croptr11.pdf

**Interstate Commerce Act.** National Archives: discriminatory rebates and preferences, Granger regulation, and creation of the ICC in 1887.  
https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/interstate-commerce-act

**Kansas railroad data.** Kansas Historical Society analysis reproducing ICC freight-rate and traffic-density data for 1890–1895 and preserving evidence on both farmer and railroad sides.  
https://www.kansashistory.gov/p/kansas-conflict-populist-versus-railroader-in-the-1890s/13274

**Farmers’ Alliance and subtreasury.** Texas State Historical Association on cooperative marketing, alternative credit, and federally backed crop-storage/loan proposals.  
https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/farmers-alliance

**Colored Farmers’ Alliance.** Texas State Historical Association on Black farmer organization, exchanges, education, mutual aid, voting-rights politics, and the violently suppressed cotton-pickers’ strike.  
https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/colored-farmers-alliance

**Mary Elizabeth Lease source audit.** Kansas History research on the “raise less corn and more hell” attribution.  
https://www.kansashistory.gov/publicat/history/2006winter_orr.pdf

**Omaha Platform, July 4, 1892.** Primary-source text for free silver at 16:1, expanded currency, graduated income tax, public railroad/telegraph/telephone ownership, land reform, and democratic/labor resolutions.  
https://teachingamericanhistory.org/document/national-peoples-party-platform-adopted-at-omaha-neb-july-4-1892/

**1892 election.** American Presidency Project: Weaver 1,029,357 popular votes, 8.5%, and 22 electoral votes.

https://www.archives.gov/electoral-college/1896

**Cross of Gold.** Library of Congress context for Bryan and the 1896 silver campaign.  
https://www.loc.gov/static/programs/national-recording-preservation-board/documents/WilliamJenningsBryan.pdf

## Accuracy guardrails

- The `$500` debt is an illustrative fixed obligation, not an average mortgage claim.
- Agricultural distress is multi-causal; do not reduce it to overproduction alone.
- The railroad section preserves both higher western freight rates and lower western traffic density.
- The ICC was a major federal regulatory step even though its early power was limited.
- Free silver is explained as a proposed monetary strategy with different effects across debtors, creditors, and wage earners, not a guaranteed cure.
- The famous “raise less corn and more hell” line is not treated as a verified Mary Elizabeth Lease quotation.
- Black farmers are organizers and political actors; the Colored Farmers’ Alliance is part of the main story, not a sidebar.
- Bryan remained a Democrat in 1896; the People’s Party endorsed him through fusion.
- Later reforms are described as echoes/adoptions of demands, not proof that Populists alone caused them.
