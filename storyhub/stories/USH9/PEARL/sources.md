# Pearl Harbor source notes

The student archive uses source IDs from `sources.ts`. Captions, collections and rights for the local images live in `media.ts` and `assets.json`. The audio and timestamped transcript live in `speech.ts`.

## Chronology and interpretation

- Use the [National Park Service chronology](https://www.nps.gov/perl/learn/historyculture/timeline.htm) with the [Navy account and source notes](https://www.history.navy.mil/about-us/leadership/director/directors-corner/h-grams/h-gram-085.html). December 7 times use 1941 Hawaiʻi Standard Time. Hours inferred for sorting a day-level record stay out of the student label.
- Ward’s sighting, firing and reporting were separate events. The Navy and NPS accounts use slightly different attack times. Approximately 6:45 a.m. and 6:51 a.m. are teaching anchors. The radar plot is a contemporary Army record of motion with no aircraft identification.
- The NPS dates the second wave at about 8:54 a.m. The [National WWII Museum overview](https://www.nationalww2museum.org/war/topics/pearl-harbor-december-7-1941) places its beginning around 8:45. The lesson labels the event approximate and notes differences across locations.
- The chart of the moorings is an edited detail of NH 114475. Rotating and cropping the original made it easier to read. Marker positions are historical moorings and general site locations. The Pacific and island overviews use Natural Earth v5.1.2, with modern shorelines and 1941 subject labels.
- The Navy’s repair history and carrier location record establish what survived. Treat claims about another attack wave as interpretation. The counterfactuals are bounded scenarios that show plausible changes in sequence.
- In Hawaiʻi, military rule and selective detention operated under local wartime authority. Executive Order 9066 later enabled exclusion and mass incarceration on the mainland. Use the NPS Honouliuli sources and the National Archives copy of the order.

## Archival media

- Period originals are credited by item and date. Nagumo and Yamamoto portraits and the first-wave photo come from records of the Imperial Japanese Navy preserved by the U.S. Navy. Dates and credits are given at the photograph. Commons records the vintage Japanese public-domain terms for the Nagumo portrait.
- Group profiles use photographs of settings or groups. The Fuchida profile shows a photograph made by a Japanese aircrew as an alternative to a portrait. The Short profile uses period radar context. These images are explicitly captioned.
- The present-day comparison uses a later U.S. Navy memorial photograph and a 1941 Navy aerial. The images have different viewpoints.
- The recording is from the [Franklin D. Roosevelt Library Digital Archives](https://archive.org/details/FranklinDelanoRooseveltDayOfInfamySpeech), whose item marks it public domain. The local 6:56 edition retains the complete address and cuts the closing concert portion. Transcript cues are aligned to this edition, with the National Archives transcript as a comparison for the prepared text.
- The typefaces Anton and Source Serif 4 are bundled under their SIL Open Font Licenses.
