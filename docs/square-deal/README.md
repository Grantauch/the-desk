# The Square Deal: teacher notes

A Progressive Era card duel for U.S. History 9. Reformers and the Machine fight over four fronts, the City, the Factory, the Market and the Vote, across ten rounds from 1900 to 1918. Every card is a real person, organization, law, scheme or court case with a one line fact on it. The page is `public/hubs/ush9-square-deal.html`, and the rules and cards live in `public/hubs/ush9-square-deal-engine.js`.

This file is for the teacher. Nothing in it appears on a student screen.

## Ways to play

- **Lead the Reformers** or **Run the Machine** against the computer. Rookie, Veteran and Legend set how sharp the computer plays.
- **Head to head** puts two students on one Chromebook. A pass screen hides each hand between turns.
- **The card library** shows all 50 cards and their facts, for review.

A game takes 15 to 20 minutes. Nothing is sent anywhere. An unfinished game is kept in that browser tab only, so a shared Chromebook starts fresh for the next student.

## The rules in short

- Each round, both sides get influence, 2 in 1900 rising to 6, and draw two cards. Unspent influence is lost.
- Exposés build Outrage on a front. Laws need a set amount of Outrage there and spend it.
- Every law on a front weakens each Machine push there by one, up to two.
- Organizers and bosses keep pushing their front every turn for a few turns. Muscle cards remove them.
- Court cards strike down laws. Amendments cannot be struck down, which is the point students should take away about the Constitution.
- Any card can be traded in for 1 influence: a speech that adds 1 Outrage for the Reformers, or greased palms that pull the front 1 for the Machine.
- After 1918, the side holding more fronts wins.

## What it teaches

The cause and effect chain of the era: exposé, public outrage, law, court challenge. Students see why muckrakers mattered, why the Keating Owen Act fell to Hammer v. Dagenhart, and why reformers pushed for amendments. The end screen tells what really happened on each front.

## Balance

The computer was tested against itself over thousands of games. With equal skill, the Reformers win a little more than half the time, which matches the era. Legend beats Rookie almost every game from either side.
