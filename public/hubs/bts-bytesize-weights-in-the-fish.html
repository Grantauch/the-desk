The team

Nobody could beat them

Jake Runyan of Cleveland and Chase Cominsky of Hermitage, Pennsylvania fished as a team on the Lake Erie Walleye Trail. A walleye tournament has one rule that matters. Teams bring back their five heaviest fish, and the heaviest bag wins.

Before 2021 they had never finished higher than 13th in the season standings. Then they started winning almost everything. Runyan said their 2021 prize money passed $300,000.

People got suspicious. Late in 2021 one of them failed a lie detector test at a Toledo tournament, and the ruling cost them about $120,000. In April 2022 police in Rossford, Ohio looked into another complaint. Nobody was charged.

The weigh in

Five pound cookie cutters

Bad weather cut the championship to one day. Plenty of teams came back with one or two fish. Before Runyan and Cominsky weighed in, the best bag was 28.18 pounds.

Their bag hit 33.91. That would have made them champions and Team of the Year, worth $28,760.

Tournament director Jason Fischer looked at the fish and saw ordinary Lake Erie walleye. He called them five pound cookie cutters. The scale said they averaged 6.78 pounds apiece.

He asked the team to set the fish aside for a photo, pressed on a belly, and felt something hard. Then he picked up a knife. A crowd of anglers was filming.

Pull the lead out

How much did the cheat buy them?

Fischer pulled ten egg shaped lead sinkers out of the fish. Eight weighed 12 ounces and two weighed 8 ounces. Tap each sinker to take it off the scale and watch where their bag lands on the leaderboard.

The fallout

From the ramp to jail

  1. Sept 30, 2022
    Disqualified on the spotOther anglers shouted at the pair to leave. Police took names but made no arrest. Steve Tyszko and Chris French were named champions.
  2. Oct 11, 2022
    The boat is seizedWildlife officers took Cominsky's Ranger boat from his home. Under the steering console they found a hidden compartment that smelled like rotten fish.
  3. Oct 12, 2022
    ChargedA grand jury charged both men with cheating, attempted grand theft and possessing criminal tools, all felonies, plus a misdemeanor for owning the extra fillets.
  4. Mar 2023
    GuiltyOn the day jury selection was supposed to start, both men pleaded guilty to cheating and the wild animals charge.
  5. May 11, 2023
    SentencedJudge Steven Gall gave them 10 days in jail, 18 months of probation, a $2,500 fine each and a three year fishing license suspension. The boat went to the state.
  6. 2024
    AuctionedOhio sold the boat for an $82,000 winning bid. The money is buying a patrol boat for wildlife officers on Lake Erie.

Was it worth it?

What they lost

  • A $100,000 boat
  • Three years without a fishing license

Starting in 2023, the top five teams at every Lake Erie Walleye Trail event can have their fish cut open. Metal detectors now sit at the weigh in.
