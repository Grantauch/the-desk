Step 1 / Day 1

Meet your veteran

Find six facts on the veteran's profile and write one short sentence for each. Partner A handles early life. Partner B handles the rank, battle and medal. The project asks these exact kinds of questions:

My project / Fact file Preview — not editable
Partner A Early life facts
Where and when was he born?

Freddie Stowers was born in Sandy Springs, South Carolina, in 1896.

Sample answer

Also asks: What did he do before the war? How and when did he join?

Partner B Battle and medal facts
When and where did he earn the Medal of Honor?

His action took place on September 28, 1918, at Hill 188 in France.

Sample answer

Also asks: What was his rank and unit? When did he receive the medal, and when did he die?

↳

What makes this strong

It includes a specific name, place and date instead of saying only “he fought in World War I.” The award date is not the same as the battle date.

Watch out

Do not confuse where the person grew up with where he fought. Check the record, not your guess.

Need an idea for where to start?

What detail tells you this veteran had a real life before he was a soldier?

  • What detail tells you this veteran had a real life before he was a soldier?
  • Can you find one exact date and one exact place that a reader could check?
  • What difference is there between when he acted bravely and when he received his medal?

Step 2 / Day 1

Crack the citation

The citation is the official explanation for the medal. Partner A chooses three difficult words from it and Partner B chooses three different words. Explain each one simply, not with a copied dictionary sentence.

My project / Hard words Preview — not editable
Partner A Three hard words
Hard word 1 from the citation

ceased

What hard word 1 means, in your own words

Stopped doing something. Here, the soldiers stopped firing.

Partner B Three more hard words
Hard word 4 from the citation

casualties

What hard word 4 means, in your own words

People killed or wounded in this fight. It does not mean everyone died.

↳

What makes this strong

Both definitions sound like a classmate speaking, and both show what the word means in the particular story.

Watch out

The word must actually appear in your veteran's citation. Partners need six different words total.

Not sure which words to choose?

Read the citation aloud. Where do you stop because a word is confusing?

  • Read the citation aloud. Where do you stop because a word is confusing?
  • If a younger student asked what this word meant, what would you say?
  • Can you explain this word using the battle in one short sentence?

Step 3 / Day 2

Write your first chapter

You do not need to write a giant essay. The project splits each chapter into three smaller boxes. Answer each in two or three sentences. Together, the boxes become paragraphs in your report.

My project / Chapter one Preview — not editable
Partner A Before the War
Where did he grow up? Who was in his family?

Freddie Stowers grew up in South Carolina. He had a wife named Pearl and a daughter named Minnie Lee.

Next boxes: What was life like before the war? How did he end up in the military?

Partner B The Day He Earned the Medal
What was the danger? What went wrong?

The enemy pretended to surrender. When the Americans came into the open, machine-gun fire struck the company.

Other boxes: Set the scene. Then tell what he did in order: first, next, then.

↳

What makes this strong

Partner A shows the person before the hero. Partner B explains the danger so that the brave action actually means something.

Watch out

Do not make up dialogue, thoughts or secret motives. Use what the source tells you.

Build a chapter around one good question

How did everyday life change when this person entered the war?

  • How did everyday life change when this person entered the war?
  • What would a reader have to know first to understand why this battle was dangerous?
  • Could you retell the action with “first,” “next” and “finally” without losing the facts?

Step 4 / Day 3

Write your second chapter

Partner A translates the three parts of the official citation into plain English. Partner B explains what happened afterward and how the veteran is remembered.

My project / Chapter two Preview — not editable
Partner A The Citation in Plain English
Part 2 of the citation, rewritten in your own words

The second part describes Stowers leading his squad against a machine gun. They destroyed the position, and he kept the attack going.

The other two boxes cover citation parts 1 and 3.

Partner B After the War
When did he get the Medal of Honor? If it came late, why?

Stowers died in 1918, but his family received the medal in 1991. His service was recognized decades after the war.

Other boxes: What happened after the fight? How is he remembered today?

↳

What makes this strong

Plain English keeps the original meaning, not its hard wording. The legacy chapter separates what happened during the war from recognition afterward.

Watch out

Paraphrasing is not changing a couple of words. Read, look away, explain, then check for accuracy.

Make the history understandable

How would you explain one sentence of the citation to a friend who missed class?

  • How would you explain one sentence of the citation to a friend who missed class?
  • What changed between the day of the action and the day of the award?
  • What memorial, record or ceremony still helps people remember this veteran?

Step 5 / Day 4

Why he matters

This is your own thinking. Each partner answers the same two reflection questions separately. If you are working alone, answer them once. There is no single “correct” opinion, but your reasoning should connect to the historical facts.

My project / Why he matters Preview — not editable
Your reflection Your words, not your partner's
Which moment in his story surprised you the most? Why?

I was surprised that Stowers received the Medal of Honor so many years after he died. I expected that kind of bravery to be recognized sooner.

Sample reflection, not a script
What does his story show you about courage, or about America in 1918?

To me, courage means acting while you know the danger is real. Stowers kept leading his men even after he was wounded.

↳

What makes this strong

The student names a specific moment and explains why it matters. The second answer connects an opinion to something the veteran actually did.

Watch out

“He was brave and important” is only a claim. Follow it with a specific example and what it made you think.

Find your own voice

What did this story make you think differently about courage?

  • What did this story make you think differently about courage?
  • Was there a moment when the veteran faced more than one possible choice?
  • What does this story reveal about both bravery and the unfairness of the time?

Step 7 / Day 7

Finish and turn in

Check your part, preview the combined paper and turn in your own work. In a team, both partners complete their own Turn In step. The hand-drawn poster must also be physically handed in.

My project / Finish Preview — not editable
Your jobs Example completion checklist
  • Six facts and six different citation words, split between partners
  • All four report chapters finished
  • Both questions answered in each partner's own reflection
  • Wall poster complete and handed in
  • Memorial screen complete with a title, drawing, caption and citation quote
  • Read the full paper preview; check names, dates and spelling
Preview full paperTurn In

These are picture labels, not working buttons. The real buttons are in your signed-in project.

↳

What makes this strong

Complete means every requirement is actually done, not just that you wrote something. A careful final read catches missing context and spelling mistakes.

Watch out

This guide cannot turn anything in. Use My project after signing in. Your partner must finish their part too.

Check it like a reader

Would someone who has never heard of your veteran understand the whole story?

  • Would someone who has never heard of your veteran understand the whole story?
  • Can you point to a source for the dates, places, actions and quotation?
  • Do the report, poster and memorial screen tell the same accurate story?
