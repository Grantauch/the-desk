Why does an authentic government transcript about Mars not count as proof of ancient Martians?

byte 01

The government really investigated it

Remote viewing means trying to describe a distant target without visiting it or receiving ordinary sensory information about it. The CIA says it began research in 1972. Other defense agencies were involved over the program’s history, and STARGATE became its best-known name. The Cold War made an apparent new way to collect secrets attractive. A government paying to investigate a claim establishes an investigation. It does not establish that the claim worked. S1

byte 02

The envelope says Mars

A surviving record dated May 22, 1984 describes a session using a sealed target envelope and spoken coordinates. Its target sheet specifies Mars at roughly one million years B.C. The subject reports impressions of large structures and other scenes; the monitor asks follow-up questions. The file is real. Those statements are reports of impressions within an experiment. The transcript contains no physical Martian sample or independent observation confirming an ancient civilization. S2

byte 03

A true document can contain an unverified claim

Suppose a police report records that a witness saw a dragon. The report can be genuine while the dragon remains unverified. You must ask two questions: did somebody actually make the statement, and was the statement accurate? The Mars transcript answers the first kind of question. Treating its release stamp as a certificate for its contents is a reasoning error. Classification tells us how a record was handled, not whether every statement inside is true.

Separate three findings

Laboratory result

Some reviewed results departed statistically from chance. What caused that pattern remained disputed.

Operational result

A useful spy report must be specific, timely and dependable. AIR found the reviewed material inadequate for that job.

byte 05

The 1995 review asked a harder question

The American Institutes for Research reviewed laboratory findings and intelligence usefulness separately. It noted statistical results beyond chance in the reviewed work, while finding that the cause was not clearly established as paranormal. Expert reviewers disagreed about the interpretation. For intelligence work, the report found descriptions too vague, inconsistent and dependent on interpretation to justify continuing operational use. Do not turn that nuanced conclusion into either “all tests were fake” or “psychic powers were proven.” S3

byte 06

The useful secret is a distinction

The CIA decided not to restore the program after the review. We have evidence for a government research effort, a striking session transcript and a critical evaluation. We do not have verified evidence of ancient Martians from this transcript. Your task is to keep those findings together without letting the strangest sentence swallow the rest of the file. Sources & limits

Historical research checked October 27, 2026. A source may establish one claim while leaving another unresolved.

S1 · CIA History Staff retrospective (2021) ↗

Confirms government remote-viewing research beginning in 1972, transfer among agencies, the 1995 review and the decision not to restore the program. Avoid relying on its simplified chronology of code names.

S2 · Mars Exploration, May 22, 1984 ↗

Primary government transcript, CIA-RDP96-00788R001900760001-9, preserved by The Black Vault. The target sheet specifies Mars at approximately one million years B.C. The source records a session, not a verified exploration.

S3 · AIR, An Evaluation of Remote Viewing (September 29, 1995) ↗

Primary commissioned evaluation, CIA-RDP96-00791R000200180006-4. Executive summary separates statistical findings, uncertain explanation and poor operational usefulness. Specialist reviewers disagreed about interpretation.
