Earth 1976 · Route 9: Go

The Vet, 1976

A go route is the simplest route in football. Run straight down the field as fast as you can. Vince Papale's route looked that simple on screen in Invincible. The real one had more stops.

Vince Papale was born in 1946 in Glenolden, Pennsylvania, just outside Philadelphia. At Interboro High School he played only one varsity football season. He went to Saint Joseph's University on a track scholarship as a pole vaulter, because the school had no football team.

He became a teacher at Interboro and coached the junior varsity. He played semipro ball for the Aston Green Knights, plus tackle games in neighborhood leagues around Philadelphia. In 1974, at 28, he made the Philadelphia Bell of the new World Football League. He told the team he was 24. He caught the first pass in the history of that league. The WFL folded in October 1975.

In 1976 the Eagles hired a new coach, Dick Vermeil. General manager Jim Murray invited Papale to a workout. He made the team at 30, the oldest rookie in NFL history who was not a kicker and had never played college football. He played in 41 of 44 games over three seasons, mostly on special teams. His teammates voted him special teams captain. A shoulder injury ended his career, and he retired in 1979.

The math of a long shot

The Odds

Most NFL players take the same route. They star in high school, earn a college scholarship, play four or five years of college football, get drafted, and sign. The NCAA counts how many players make it through each step.

NCAA figures for 2018 and 2019: 1,006,013 high school football players, 73,712 NCAA players, 254 players drafted. Each year's draft class is compared with about a quarter of the high school total, one graduating class, which comes to about one drafted player for every 1,000 seniors.

    Route 2 · SlantEarth 1952Los Angeles Rams

    Night Train Lane

    A slant is three steps and a hard cut. Lane cut his bus ride short and walked into the Rams office.

    Dick Lane played one season of junior college football in Nebraska, then spent four years in the Army. At Fort Ord in California he caught 18 touchdown passes for the base team in 1951.

    Out of the Army in 1952, he worked at an aircraft plant in Los Angeles, lifting heavy sheets of metal out of a bin and feeding them into a press. His bus to work passed the office of the Los Angeles Rams, the defending NFL champions. One day he got off, walked in with a scrapbook of old newspaper clippings, and asked for a tryout.

    The Rams tried him at receiver and then moved him to defense. In 12 games that fall he intercepted 14 passes. Teams now play 17 games, and nobody has passed him. He finished his career with the Detroit Lions from 1960 to 1965 and went into the Hall of Fame in 1974.

    Michigan connection: six seasons with the Lions

    "Lane came out here to make the ball club. Well, last night he got himself a job."

    Rams coach Joe Stydahar, after Lane's first scrimmage, August 1952

    Route 0 · HitchEarth 1955Baltimore Colts

    Johnny Unitas

    A hitch runs five yards, stops, and turns back for the ball. His hometown team stopped Unitas cold, and he turned around for one more throw.

    Earth 1955 editionPittsburgh, PennsylvaniaPay: $6 a game

    The Pittsburgh Steelers drafted Johnny Unitas out of Louisville in the ninth round in 1955, the 102nd pick. He had grown up in Pittsburgh. The team cut him before he ever played in a preseason game, and he hitchhiked home.

    He took a construction job as a pile driver to support his wife and the baby they had on the way. On weekends he played for the Bloomfield Rams, a sandlot team on a field beneath the Bloomfield Bridge, for $6 a game. He played quarterback and safety and did the punting. The field was covered in rocks and broken glass.

    In 1956 the Baltimore Colts needed a backup quarterback. Their long distance call to Pittsburgh cost about 80 cents. Two years later Unitas led the Colts to the 1958 NFL title in overtime against the New York Giants, a game people still call the Greatest Game Ever Played.

    He made ten Pro Bowls. His number 19 was retired by the Colts, and he went into the Hall of Fame in 1979.

    "We sort of made up the plays as we went along."

    Johnny Unitas on the Bloomfield Rams, as told to NFL Films

    Route 3 · ComebackEarth 1969Pittsburgh Steelers

    Rocky Bleier

    A comeback runs deep, then turns back toward the ball. Bleier went to war and came back to the same team.

    The Steelers took Rocky Bleier with the 417th pick in the 1968 draft. He had been team captain at Notre Dame. After his rookie season the Army drafted him, and in May 1969 he shipped out to Vietnam with the 196th Light Infantry Brigade.

    On August 20, 1969, his unit came under attack near Hiep Duc. A bullet went through his thigh, and a grenade blast tore into his right leg and foot. Doctors removed more than 100 pieces of shrapnel. In a hospital in Tokyo they told him he would not play football again. He received the Bronze Star and the Purple Heart.

    While he recovered, a postcard arrived from Steelers owner Art Rooney. Bleier reported to camp in 1970 at about 180 pounds and could not walk without pain. The team kept him on injured reserve. By 1974 he was a starting running back. He won Super Bowls IX, X, XIII and XIV, and he caught a touchdown pass from Terry Bradshaw in Super Bowl XIII.

    Route 4 · CurlEarth 1994St. Louis Rams

    Kurt Warner

    A curl runs upfield, then stops in open space and waits for the ball. Warner waited five years.

    Kurt Warner sat on the bench at Northern Iowa until his senior year. No team drafted him in 1994. The Green Bay Packers brought him to training camp and cut him.

    He went back to Cedar Falls, Iowa, and stocked shelves on the night shift at a Hy-Vee grocery store for $5.50 an hour. In 1995 he signed with the Iowa Barnstormers of the Arena Football League and played there through 1997. The St. Louis Rams signed him in 1998 and sent him to play for the Amsterdam Admirals in NFL Europe.

    In the 1999 preseason, Rams starter Trent Green tore his ACL. Warner took over and threw 41 touchdown passes. He won the NFL MVP award. In Super Bowl XXXIV he threw for 414 yards, a Super Bowl record at the time, and won that game's MVP too. He went into the Hall of Fame in 2017. Hollywood told his story in American Underdog in 2021.

    Route 1 · FlatEarth 2001New Orleans Saints

    Michael Lewis

    The flat stays short and close to the sideline. Lewis never left home. His delivery route ran past the stadium.

    Michael Lewis grew up in New Orleans and never went to college. He drove a delivery truck for a Budweiser distributor, and his route ran a short distance from the Superdome, where the Saints played. Fans would later call him the Beer Man.

    He kept playing football in semipro leagues and in arena football as a kick returner. In 2000 his hometown Saints gave him a tryout and a spot on the practice squad. He also played for the Rhein Fire in NFL Europe. In 2001, at 29, he made the Saints roster as a return man.

    In 2002 he returned kickoffs for 1,807 yards and punts for 625. The total, 2,432 return yards in one season, set an NFL record. He made the Pro Bowl and first team All Pro. The Saints put him in their Hall of Fame in 2015.

    "I took the long route to get here."

    Michael Lewis, Saints training camp, August 2006

    Route 5 · OutEarth 2001New England Patriots

    Stephen Neal

    An out route breaks hard toward the sideline. Neal broke out of a different sport completely.

    Stephen Neal never played college football. At Cal State Bakersfield he was a heavyweight wrestler. He went 156 and 10 and won two NCAA titles. In the 1999 NCAA final he beat Brock Lesnar, who later became a WWE champion. That same year Neal won the world freestyle championship.

    He had not played football since high school in San Diego when the New England Patriots signed him in July 2001 after a special workout. He said the helmet felt like it weighed 20 pounds. The Patriots cut him in August. He spent that fall on the Philadelphia Eagles practice squad, and in December the Patriots signed him back.

    He learned to play guard and stayed in New England through 2010. He started 81 of his 86 games and won Super Bowls XXXVI, XXXVIII and XXXIX.

    Route 6 · DigEarth 2003San Diego Chargers

    Antonio Gates

    A dig cuts flat across the middle of the field. Gates crossed over from the basketball court.

    Antonio Gates starred in football and basketball at Detroit Central High School. In college he chose basketball. After short stops at Michigan State and Eastern Michigan, he landed at Kent State. In 2002 he helped the Golden Flashes reach the Elite Eight of the NCAA tournament.

    He never played a down of college football. After his last basketball season in 2003 he worked out for NFL scouts. The San Diego Chargers signed him as an undrafted free agent, and he played 16 seasons for them.

    He caught 116 touchdown passes, an NFL record for a tight end, and made eight Pro Bowls. In 2025 he became the first player in the Pro Football Hall of Fame who never played college football.

    Michigan connection: Detroit Central High School

    "NFL teams and scouts will never look at college basketball players the same again."

    Antonio Gates, Hall of Fame speech, August 2, 2025

    Route 8 · PostEarth 2018Philadelphia Eagles

    Jordan Mailata

    The post goes deep, the longest throw on the tree. Mailata's route started about 9,900 miles away.

    Jordan Mailata grew up in Bankstown, a suburb of Sydney, Australia, playing rugby league. He came up through the South Sydney Rabbitohs junior teams but never played a top level NRL game. He watched one NFL game a year, the Super Bowl.

    In 2018 the NFL invited him to its International Player Pathway program, which trains athletes from other countries at IMG Academy in Florida. He had never played a down of American football. He stood 6 feet 8 and weighed 346 pounds. The Philadelphia Eagles, Papale's old team, traded up in the seventh round and took him with the 233rd pick.

    He spent two years learning to block. In 2021 he won the starting left tackle job and signed a four year, $64 million extension. In 2024 he signed another for three years and $66 million. In February 2025 he won Super Bowl LIX with the Eagles.

    "I thought it was an instruction manual to build a spaceship."

    Mailata on his first NFL playbook, The Ringer, 2023
    Route 7 · CornerEarth 2023Dallas Cowboys

    Brandon Aubrey

    A corner route angles for the far pylon, away from everybody else. Aubrey took an angle nobody had tried.

    Brandon Aubrey won a national championship playing soccer at Notre Dame. Toronto FC took him in the first round of the 2017 MLS draft, but he never played a game for them. After a lower league club released him in 2018, he went to work as a software engineer at GM Financial.

    He had never kicked a football in a game. One day he and his wife Jenn bought a kicking tee and went to a high school field. He lined up a 60 yard field goal and made it. He found a kicking coach, Bryan Egan, and kept training while he worked. He kicked for the Birmingham Stallions of the USFL, and the Dallas Cowboys signed him in 2023.

    He made the first 35 field goals of his NFL career, a league record. In 2024 he made 14 field goals of 50 yards or longer, the most ever in one season. In September 2025 he hit a 64 yarder against the Giants to force overtime, then kicked the winner.

    Meanwhile, in other universes

    Other Sports

    Football does not own the wild route. Hockey and baseball each have a story strange enough to beat anything on the tree.

    Earth 2020 · Hockey

    The Zamboni Driver

    Dave Ayres was 42 and worked maintenance and drove the Zamboni for the Toronto Marlies, the Maple Leafs' minor league team. He was also the emergency backup goalie for NHL games in Toronto, available to either team. Fifteen years earlier he had a kidney transplant.

    On February 22, 2020, both Carolina Hurricanes goalies, James Reimer and Petr Mrazek, got hurt against the Leafs. Ayres pulled on a Carolina jersey with number 90 and went in during the second period. The first two shots went in. He stopped the next eight. Carolina won 6 to 3. He was the first emergency goalie ever to win an NHL game and the oldest goalie to win his regular season debut.

    Earth 1999 · Baseball

    The Science Teacher

    Arm injuries ended Jim Morris's minor league career in the late 1980s. He became a physical science teacher and baseball coach at Reagan County High School in Big Lake, Texas. His players made him a deal. If they won the district title, he would try out for a major league team. They won.

    In June 1999, at 35, he went to a Tampa Bay Devil Rays tryout and threw 98 miles per hour, about 12 faster than he had thrown ten years before. On September 18, 1999, he struck out Royce Clayton of the Texas Rangers on four pitches. Disney told his story in The Rookie in 2002, four years before Invincible. Like Papale, he was a teacher.

    The story behind the story

    Behind the ink

    A movie changes a life into a story. An illustrated history makes choices too. This page should show you its choices.

    First came the routes.

    The first Route Tree followed ten careers through a comic-book multiverse: cuts, phone calls, injuries and doors nobody expected to open. It began with Invincible and a problem historians know well: a memorable version of a life can crowd out the real one.

    Then came a different kind of ink.

    The visual breakthrough was a player listening to a coach. A believable exchange mattered more than a heroic pose. That became the rule for this revision: documentary reality, graphic-novel execution. Real photographs establish people and places; expressive contours, selective crosshatching and fine halftone give the artwork its surface.

    The evidence stayed in the frame.

    The opening, Papale, Warner and Bleier became the first four illustrated scenes. Authentic photographs now anchor all ten NFL stories. Original sources remain separate, later portraits carry dates, and imagined scenes say so. Images whose provenance could not be verified were left out. The source controls let you challenge the finished artwork instead of simply trusting it.

    Three images. Three different claims.

    A real person, at a particular time.
