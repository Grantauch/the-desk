23 MonthsMichael Vick

In 2006 Michael Vick was the most exciting player in football and held the richest contract the NFL had ever handed out. A year later federal agents were digging up dog graves behind a house he owned in Virginia. He went to prison, came out broke, and in 2010 played the best game of his life on Monday Night Football.

He lost almost everything. Who decided he could have it back?

Newport News, Virginia · 1980 to 2000

Bad Newz

Michael Vick was born in 1980 and grew up in Ridley Circle, a public housing project in Newport News, Virginia. Locals called the city Bad Newz. He played quarterback at Warwick High School for coach Tommie Reamon.

At Virginia Tech he sat out 1998 as a redshirt. In 1999 he took over as a freshman and the Hokies went 11 and 0 in the regular season. He finished third in the Heisman Trophy vote, which almost never happens for a freshman.

On January 4, 2000, Virginia Tech played Florida State for the national title in the Sugar Bowl. The Hokies lost 46 to 29. Vick threw for 225 yards and ran for 97 more. Nobody who watched forgot it.

Atlanta Falcons · 2001 to 2006

The No. 1 pick

Atlanta traded up with San Diego to take Vick first overall in the 2001 draft. San Diego used the fifth pick on LaDainian Tomlinson. Vick was the first Black quarterback ever chosen first overall.

Defenses had never seen a quarterback that fast. On December 1, 2002 he ran for 173 yards at Minnesota, a record for a quarterback, and won the game with a 46 yard run in overtime. A month later Atlanta won a playoff game at Lambeau Field. The Packers had never lost a home playoff game before that night.

He was on the cover of Madden NFL 2004. Kids who played it still talk about how unfair he was in the game. In December 2004 the Falcons signed him to a 10 year deal worth up to $130 million, the richest contract in league history at the time. Nike and Coca Cola paid him to sell their products.

In 2006 he ran for 1,039 yards. No quarterback had ever reached 1,000 in a season.

1915 Moonlight Road · Surry County, Virginia

The house on Moonlight Road

In 2001, the year he was drafted, Vick paid for a 15 acre property in rural Surry County, about an hour from where he grew up. His friends built kennels and sheds behind a white brick house. From 2002 on it was the home of a dogfighting operation they called Bad Newz Kennels.

On April 20, 2007 police in Hampton arrested Vick's cousin Davon Boddie on drug charges. Boddie gave 1915 Moonlight Road as his address. Five days later officers searched the property. They found dogs chained to car axles, just out of reach of each other, along with equipment for training and fighting them. Animal control officers took 66 dogs away, most of them pit bulls.

Vick was the face of the Falcons and one of the best known athletes in America. His first move was to say it had nothing to do with him.

Vick told reporters he was rarely at the house and blamed relatives who stayed there. At the NFL draft that week he told Commissioner Roger Goodell he was not involved.

Leavenworth

Vick turned himself in early, three weeks before his sentencing. He was moved to Leavenworth in Kansas, one of the first federal prisons ever built.

The money went faster than the career. The Falcons went to court to get back bonus money. Every major sponsor dropped him. In July 2008, from prison, he filed for bankruptcy.

He chose Chapter 11 instead of Chapter 7. Chapter 7 would have wiped out most of what he owed. Chapter 11 meant paying it back out of whatever he earned in the future, if he ever earned anything again.

FedExField, Landover, Maryland · November 15, 2010

Monday Night

He spent 2009 as a backup. In the first game of 2010, starter Kevin Kolb got a concussion and Vick took his job. In Week 10 Philadelphia went to Washington on Monday Night Football. On the first play from scrimmage Vick threw an 88 yard touchdown to DeSean Jackson. Philadelphia won 59 to 28.

No player in NFL history had thrown for 300 yards, run for 50, thrown four touchdowns and run for two in the same game. The Associated Press named him Comeback Player of the Year. In July 2011 Nike signed him again. A month later the Eagles gave him a six year deal worth up to $100 million. A sports business reporter at CNBC called it the first time a brand had ever come back to an athlete it dropped.

Norfolk, Virginia · 2017 to 2026

Where it landed

Vick played his last NFL snaps for Pittsburgh in 2015 and officially retired in February 2017. That June the Falcons held a ceremony to honor him. He did not sign a contract to retire as a Falcon. Owner Arthur Blank said Vick would "certainly be considered" for the team's Ring of Honor.

In February 2018 a judge closed his bankruptcy case. He had paid back more than $17 million. A consultant on the case said a debtor pays back almost all of what he owes in maybe one case out of a hundred.

On December 20, 2024 Norfolk State University hired him as head football coach, across the harbor from Ridley Circle. His first team went 1 and 11. His second team opened 2026 with a 56 to 0 win and was 4 and 3 by mid October.
