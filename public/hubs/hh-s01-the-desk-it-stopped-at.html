A government document can be real, appalling, and still be misused. Operation Northwoods teaches the harder skill: keep the proposal and its status in the same sentence.

March 13, 1962

The page

A memorandum went from General Lyman L. Lemnitzer, Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff, to Secretary of Defense Robert McNamara. The subject was Justification for US Military Intervention in Cuba.

It was classified for thirty-five years. Today it is declassified and publicly readable.

An ordinary page.
An extraordinary proposal.
Document identity

Start with the face of the record.

DateMarch 13, 1962
FromJoint Chiefs of Staff, signed by Chairman Lyman L. Lemnitzer
ToSecretary of Defense Robert McNamara
Subject“Justification for US Military Intervention in Cuba”
StatusTop Secret; recommendation with an attached staff paper
What was attached

An enclosure of proposed pretexts.

The attached staff paper described operations that could create the appearance that Cuba had attacked the United States or its neighbors. Related files associate the project with the code word “Northwoods.”

How anyone can verify it

The paper is publicly readable.

The National Archives holds the released record, and the National Security Archive at George Washington University publishes the memorandum and enclosure. Its genuineness does not depend on an internet screenshot.

After the Bay of Pigs

Where it came from

The failed April 1961 Bay of Pigs invasion did not end Washington’s goal of removing Fidel Castro. In late 1961, Operation Mongoose organized a new covert effort.

Edward Lansdale asked the Joint Chiefs for possible pretexts that could justify U.S. military intervention. A staff group produced options. The process was routine. The content was not.

Cuba · Florida · Guantánamo
April 1961

Bay of Pigs failed in three days.

A CIA-backed Cuban exile force landed on April 17 and was defeated and captured. The public humiliation did not end the administration’s objective of removing Fidel Castro’s government.

November 1961

Operation Mongoose reorganized the effort.

The Cuba Project combined sabotage, propaganda, intelligence collection, and planning for possible American intervention under Brigadier General Edward Lansdale.

The request

Lansdale asked for pretexts.

The request went to the Joint Chiefs in writing. Their Caribbean Survey Group developed the response. The process was routine staff work; the content that moved through it was not.

Why this mattersNorthwoods was neither an isolated internet artifact nor an approved national policy. It was an official staff response inside an established covert program.
The enclosure

What it proposed

The attached paper proposed incidents that could make Cuba appear to be the aggressor. The document’s own language matters because internet retellings often sharpen or flatten the details.

Some proposals contemplated staged incidents. Some contemplated real harm. The question is not whether the paper is disturbing. It is what status each idea actually reached.

Keep the status attached.
The proposal set

The paper escalated across several kinds of incident.

  • Staged attacks and sabotage at the American base at Guantánamo.
  • Destroying an American ship in Cuban waters and blaming Cuba.
  • Sinking a refugee vessel, real or simulated.
  • A terror campaign in Florida or Washington attributed to Cuba.
  • A simulated attack on a civil airliner using a substituted drone.
  • A fabricated Cuban attack on a neighboring state to build regional support.
The airliner correction

The document specifies a drone substitution.

Popular summaries often say the paper proposed shooting down a passenger aircraft full of Americans. The written scenario boarded passengers under aliases, diverted the real aircraft, and destroyed an unmanned substitute. Other proposals contemplated real casualties, so this correction neither sanitizes nor exaggerates the paper.

Status stays attachedEvery item was proposed, signed, and forwarded. No approval, tasking order, or matching operation appears in the released record.

Who signed it

Lyman Lemnitzer

This was not an unsigned brainstorm left in a drawer. Lemnitzer, the senior uniformed military officer in the United States, signed the memorandum transmitting and recommending the paper to the Secretary of Defense.

That does not make every line adopted U.S. policy. It does make the proposal institutionally serious.

SIGNED ≠ APPROVED
The office

Senior adviser, not operational commander.

The Chairman of the Joint Chiefs was the highest-ranking uniformed officer and principal military adviser to the President and Secretary of Defense. The Joint Chiefs could assess, plan, and recommend; they could not independently order an operation.

The legal chain

Command authority ran around the Joint Chiefs.

PresidentCould authorize covert action and direct the use of force.
Secretary of DefenseCould approve, reject, or direct military work.
Combatant commandersCould execute an authorized operation.
Joint ChiefsAdvised the President and Secretary; recommended but did not command.
Two truthsThe signature does not make Northwoods an order. It does show that the collective senior military leadership formally recommended the paper for planning purposes.
Robert McNamara

The desk it stopped at

The memorandum reached the Secretary of Defense. The released record contains no approval that turned the Northwoods proposals into operations, and historians have found no evidence that these proposed incidents were carried out.

That negative claim has a limit: absence in a declassified record is evidence, not magical proof of every possible absence.

The paper moved.
The operation did not.
Robert McNamara

The Secretary of Defense could have created the next document.

An approval, directive, or request for operational planning would normally leave a record. No such Northwoods document appears in the declassified collection.

March 16, 1962

The presidential meeting points away from force.

Three days after the memorandum, Lemnitzer attended a Cuba-policy meeting where the record has President Kennedy stating that military force was not what they were discussing.

A tempting inference

Why Lemnitzer was not reappointed remains unsettled.

His chairmanship ended later in 1962, after which he received major commands in Europe and NATO. Northwoods is one plausible factor, but no released document establishes the reason.

1962 to 1997

Thirty-five years

The memorandum remained classified until 1997, when it surfaced through the Assassination Records Review Board’s release of Kennedy-era records.

Northwoods was not released because someone decided to tell a Northwoods story. It emerged because a legal records-opening process reached the file.

Closed drawers.
Thirty-five years.
The release chain

An unrelated records law opened the drawer.

1962The memorandum is classified and filed.
1992Congress passes the JFK Assassination Records Collection Act and creates an independent review board.
Nov. 18, 1997Northwoods appears in a release of roughly 1,500 pages from adjacent Kennedy-era files.
2001James Bamford’s Body of Secrets brings the paper to broad public attention.
What “declassified” means

Released, not leaked.

The document became public through a law and review process. Declassification changes who may read a record; it does not by itself prove every interpretation made about that record.

What happened online

The claim drifts

One internet version keeps the terrifying proposals and drops their status: the government planned this, therefore the government does this. Another keeps the rejection and drops the seriousness: nothing happened, therefore the document means nothing.

Both versions get easier to share by deleting a load-bearing fact.

True fact + missing status = worse claim
The drift mechanism

Each retelling drops one status word.

DocumentedA signed proposal exists.
Compressed“The government planned it.”
Inflated“The government was going to do it.”
Generalized“The government carries out operations like this.”
Detached“This later event must also have been staged.”
Why this misinformation survives

The citation is genuine.

A real archival link makes the whole package feel verified. But the document only verifies the statement written on its face; it cannot automatically verify the extra status or unrelated conclusion added during retelling.

Transferable skillDo not ask only whether a claim is true or false. Ask where the evidence stops carrying it.

What it establishes

Do not shrink it

The senior military leadership of the United States put its name on a paper proposing deception to create political justification for war. Some proposals contemplated real harm.

A rejected proposal can still tell you something important about what powerful people considered thinkable.

REAL · SIGNED · SERIOUS
Supported at full strength

Deception was formally thinkable at the top of the military.

The paper establishes that deliberate deception of the American public to create justification for war entered the range of options that could be drafted, staffed, signed by the Joint Chiefs, and forwarded to the Secretary of Defense.

It passed every internal review it encountered before reaching McNamara. That institutional fact remains serious even though the next step never occurred.

A second finding

The record could be opened.

Congressional law and an independent review board brought the document into public view. It was not rumor, anonymous testimony, or an unverifiable leak.

What it does not establish

Do not inflate it

Northwoods does not establish that the proposed incidents were approved or carried out. It does not prove that every later suspicious event was staged. It does not give a blank check to replace evidence with possibility.

The document is strongest when you refuse to make it carry more weight than it can.

NEW CLAIM?
NEW EVIDENCE.
Negative evidence

Proving absence requires a method.

An approval or operation should have produced directives, tasking orders, unit records, and after-action reports. A collection complete enough to contain the proposal but none of those later artifacts is strong evidence that the chain stopped. It is still an argument from an expected record, not magical proof that no unknown paper could exist.

Three unsupported conclusions
  • No record establishes personal approval by Kennedy.
  • No record establishes why Lemnitzer was not reappointed.
  • No Northwoods document can prove an unrelated later event.
Two shapes of secrecy · Compare Northwoods to Roswell

Hidden event and contained proposal are not the same structure.

RoswellThe government issued a false public explanation while a real classified balloon program remained hidden. Secrecy concealed something that happened.
NorthwoodsThe government made no public statement about a classified proposal that stopped before approval. Secrecy contained something that did not happen.

Before transferring an explanation from one secret case to another, identify what was said publicly, what was true privately, what actually occurred, and where the gap lies.

The whole answer

What the document is

Operation Northwoods is a real, signed, declassified proposal by the Joint Chiefs of Staff to manufacture pretexts for war with Cuba.

The released record contains no approval of those proposals and no evidence that they were carried out. If your sentence loses either half, your sentence got worse.

Keep both halves.
The finished historical sentence

Operation Northwoods was a genuine, signed proposal by the Joint Chiefs to manufacture pretexts for war with Cuba. It was never approved and no proposal in it was carried out.

Neither half cancels the other. Treating a proposal as policy inflates the evidence; treating a rejected proposal as meaningless shrinks it. Historical maturity is the ability to keep both findings in the same sentence.

Note repair

Check the version in your head.

Open any statement that resembles your notes. Repair it with the status and source boundary still attached.

“Operation Northwoods is a hoax or internet myth.”

It is a genuine March 13, 1962 Joint Chiefs memorandum signed by Chairman Lyman Lemnitzer, declassified on November 18, 1997, held by the National Archives, and published by the National Security Archive.

Check Section 1
“The United States carried out Operation Northwoods.”

The paper proposed a set of manufactured pretexts. No approval, directive, tasking order, matching operation, or after-action record appears in the released files.

Check Sections 3 and 5
“The plan was to shoot down an airliner full of Americans.”

The airliner scenario describes substituting an unmanned drone for the passenger aircraft. Other proposals in the same paper contemplated real casualties, so both facts belong in an accurate description.

Check Section 3 and the annex
“Kennedy fired Lemnitzer over Northwoods.”

Lemnitzer was not reappointed as chairman and later received major NATO commands. No released document establishes Northwoods as the reason.

Check Sections 5 and 9
“The document was leaked.”

It was released through the Assassination Records Review Board under the 1992 JFK records law, in a batch of roughly 1,500 pages.

Check Section 6
“Since nothing was carried out, the document does not matter.”

It establishes that the senior military leadership formally considered, staffed, signed, and transmitted proposals to deceive the public into supporting a war. Rejection limits its status; it does not erase what was considered.

Check Section 8
End of record

Close the file.

The mature answer is not “fake” and not “they did it.” It is the whole record, with the boundary still attached.

Uncertainty rule

Attractive inferences stay labeled.

The page does not claim Kennedy personally read the paper, that Northwoods caused Lemnitzer’s non-reappointment, or that every popular summary of the airliner proposal matches the document. Those conclusions exceed the released record.
