1955 · Route 0: Hitch

Johnny Unitas

A hitch runs five yards, stops, and turns back for the ball. His hometown team stopped Unitas cold, and he turned around for one more throw.

The Pittsburgh Steelers drafted Johnny Unitas out of Louisville in the ninth round in 1955, the 102nd pick. He had grown up in Pittsburgh. The team cut him before he ever played in a preseason game, and he hitchhiked home.

He took a construction job as a pile driver to support his wife and the baby they had on the way. On weekends he played for the Bloomfield Rams, a sandlot team on a field beneath the Bloomfield Bridge, for only a few dollars a game. He played quarterback and safety and did the punting. The field was covered in rocks and broken glass.

In 1956 the Baltimore Colts needed a backup quarterback. Their long distance call to Pittsburgh cost about 80 cents. Two years later Unitas led the Colts to the 1958 NFL title in overtime against the New York Giants, a game people still call the Greatest Game Ever Played.

He made ten Pro Bowls. His number 19 was retired by the Colts, and he went into the Hall of Fame in 1979.

"We sort of made up the plays as we went along."

Johnny Unitas on the Bloomfield Rams, as told to NFL Films

1976 · Route 9: Go

Vince Papale

A go route is the simplest route in football. Run straight down the field as fast as you can. Vince Papale's route looked that simple on screen in Invincible. The real one had more stops.

Vince Papale was born in 1946 in Glenolden, Pennsylvania, just outside Philadelphia. At Interboro High School he played only one varsity football season. He went to Saint Joseph's University on a track scholarship as a pole vaulter, because the school had no football team.

He became a teacher at Interboro and coached the junior varsity. He played semipro ball for the Aston Green Knights, plus tackle games in neighborhood leagues around Philadelphia. In 1974, at 28, he made the Philadelphia Bell of the new World Football League. He told the team he was 24. He caught the first pass in the history of that league. The WFL folded in October 1975.

In 1976 the Eagles hired a new coach, Dick Vermeil. General manager Jim Murray invited Papale to a workout. He made the team at 30, the oldest rookie in NFL history who was not a kicker and had never played college football. He played in 41 of 44 games over three seasons, mostly on special teams. His teammates voted him special teams captain. A shoulder injury ended his career, and he retired in 1979.

Invincible compresses this route. The movie omits the Philadelphia Bell seasons and turns an invited workout into an open-tryout story. A clearer underdog plot is not the same thing as a complete historical account.

2001 · Route 1: Flat

Michael Lewis

The flat stays short and close to the sideline. Lewis never left home. His delivery route ran past the stadium.

Michael Lewis grew up in New Orleans and never went to college. He drove a delivery truck for a Budweiser distributor, and his route ran a short distance from the Superdome, where the Saints played. Fans would later call him the Beer Man.

He kept playing football in semipro leagues and in arena football as a kick returner. In 2000 his hometown Saints gave him a tryout and a spot on the practice squad. He also played for the Rhein Fire in NFL Europe. In 2001, at 29, he made the Saints roster as a return man.

In 2002 he returned kickoffs for 1,807 yards and punts for 625. The total, 2,432 return yards in one season, set an NFL record. He made the Pro Bowl and first team All Pro. The Saints put him in their Hall of Fame in 2015.

"I took the long route to get here."

Michael Lewis, Saints training camp, August 2006

2003 · Route 6: Dig

Antonio Gates

A dig cuts flat across the middle of the field. Gates crossed over from the basketball court.

Antonio Gates starred in football and basketball at Detroit Central High School. In college he chose basketball. After short stops at Michigan State and Eastern Michigan, he landed at Kent State. In 2002 he helped the Golden Flashes reach the Elite Eight of the NCAA tournament.

He never played a down of college football. After his last basketball season in 2003 he worked out for NFL scouts. The San Diego Chargers signed him as an undrafted free agent, and he played 16 seasons for them.

He caught 116 touchdown passes, an NFL record for a tight end, and made eight Pro Bowls. In 2025 he became the first player in the Pro Football Hall of Fame who never played college football.

"NFL teams and scouts will never look at college basketball players the same again."

Antonio Gates, Hall of Fame speech, August 2, 2025

2018 · Route 8: Post

Jordan Mailata

The post goes deep, the longest throw on the tree. Mailata's route started about 9,900 miles away.

Jordan Mailata grew up in Bankstown, a suburb of Sydney, Australia, playing rugby league. He came up through the South Sydney Rabbitohs junior teams but never played a top level NRL game. He watched one NFL game a year, the Super Bowl.

In 2018 the NFL invited him to its International Player Pathway program, which trains athletes from other countries at IMG Academy in Florida. He had never played a down of American football. He stood 6 feet 8 and weighed 346 pounds. The Philadelphia Eagles, Papale's old team, traded up in the seventh round and took him with the 233rd pick.

He spent two years learning to block. In 2021 he won the starting left tackle job and signed a four year, $64 million extension. In 2024 he signed another for three years and $66 million. In February 2025 he won Super Bowl LIX with the Eagles.

"I thought it was an instruction manual to build a spaceship."

Mailata on his first NFL playbook, The Ringer, 2023

Read the picture like a historian

Look beyond the surface.

A later portrait is not a photograph of the earlier struggle.

The Unitas portrait was made in 1996. The sandlot illustration imagines 1955. The biography supports construction work and semipro football, but the exact pose, clothes, field and young likeness are interpreted.

What can each image actually establish?

The photograph provides a reliable later likeness. It does not record his construction shift or the 1955 practice. The reconstruction visualizes a researched possibility; it cannot prove that this particular bench, teammate or moment existed.

“The source establishes ___. The storyteller has added ___. To confirm ___, I would need ___.” Use dates and the image label before drawing a conclusion.

One cross-sport connection

Jim Morris: the science teacher

Arm injuries ended Jim Morris's minor league career in the late 1980s. He became a physical science teacher and baseball coach at Reagan County High School in Big Lake, Texas. His players made him a deal. If they won the district title, he would try out for a major league team. They won.

In June 1999, at 35, he went to a Tampa Bay Devil Rays tryout and threw 98 miles per hour, about 12 faster than he had thrown ten years before. On September 18, 1999, he struck out Royce Clayton of the Texas Rangers on four pitches. Disney told his story in The Rookie in 2002, four years before Invincible. Like Papale, he was a teacher.

Connection: Like Papale, Morris was a teacher. An agreement with his players opened a chance, but he still had to demonstrate the skill.
