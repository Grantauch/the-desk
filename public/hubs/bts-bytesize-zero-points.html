The sport

From a Bronx rec room to the Olympics

  1. Aug 11, 1973
    DJ Kool Herc stretches the drum breaks of funk records at a party at 1520 Sedgwick Avenue in the Bronx. Dancers build moves for the breaks.
  2. 2018
    Breaking appears at the Youth Olympic Games in Buenos Aires.
  3. Dec 2020
    The IOC adds breaking to Paris 2024 to reach younger viewers.
  4. Oct 2023
    Los Angeles leaves breaking off the 2028 program. Paris will be its only Olympics for now.

The rules

Nine judges, two rounds, one vote each

In a battle, two dancers take turns to music the DJ picks live. Neither one knows the song until it starts. After every round, each of nine judges votes for one dancer. In the group stage a battle had two rounds, so 18 votes were up for grabs.

The athlete

How a lecturer got to Paris

Rachael Gunn started breaking in her mid twenties. In 2017 she earned a PhD in cultural studies at Macquarie University in Sydney, writing about women in Sydney's mostly male breaking scene. By 2024 she taught there and competed as Raygun.

Every continent got Olympic spots. Oceania got one for b-girls. In October 2023 Gunn won the Oceania championship in Sydney, beating Molly Chapman in the final in front of nine international judges.

She followed the rules exactly. The rules sent the best b-girl in a small region to face the best in the world.

The pile on

Rumor or record?

Within hours the kangaroo hop was a meme. Within days an anonymous Change.org petition with more than 45,000 signatures claimed she had rigged her way in. Australia's Olympic committee answered with a list of facts. Tap each claim.

After Paris

Number one, then gone

  1. Sept 2024
    The world federation's rankings list Raygun as the number one b-girl on Earth. Olympic events did not count and many top breakers' points had expired.
  2. Nov 6, 2024
    She retires from competition. "I just didn't have any control over how people saw me or who I was."
  3. Feb 2026
    She leaves her teaching job at Macquarie University.
  4. Sept 2026
    Netflix releases Untold Raygun: Breaking Badly. In the trailer she says she feared "the whole world could laugh at me."
