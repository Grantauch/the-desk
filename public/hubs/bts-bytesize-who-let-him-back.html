Byte 2 · The dogs

What happened to the dogs

Before 2007, dogs taken from fighting rings were usually put down as a group. People assumed they were too dangerous to save. The Vick case was the first time a court agreed to judge each dog one at a time.

A court appointed guardian, law professor Rebecca Huss, oversaw where every dog went. Vick paid $928,073 for their care. Press each step to follow them.

66

Dogs removed from 1915 Moonlight Road on April 25, 2007.

In the case Best Friends, Utah Seven other groups Did not survive

The dogs became known as the Vicktory dogs. One of them, Jonny Justice, became a therapy dog who sat with kids while they practiced reading out loud. In April 2009 the Humane Society of the United States and Best Friends Animal Society announced that dogs seized from fighting busts should be evaluated one by one instead of put down as a group. The last two known survivors, Jonny Justice and Frodo, died in December 2021.

Byte 3 · The law

The rules changed around him

Eight days after police searched Vick's property, the president signed a law that had been moving through Congress for years. Federal dogfighting law got tougher three times in seven years. Vick was part of the last push.

2007

Animal Fighting Prohibition Enforcement Act

Signed by President George W. Bush on May 3. Moving animals across state lines to fight becomes a felony with up to 3 years in prison.

2008

Farm Bill amendment

Congress raises the maximum federal sentence from 3 years to 5.

2011

Vick on Capitol Hill

Vick lobbies Congress for a bill to punish people who pay to watch dogfights. He says he wants to be part of the solution.

2014

Animal Fighting Spectator Prohibition Act

Signed by President Barack Obama as part of the Farm Bill. Attending a fight becomes a federal crime. Bringing a child under 16 becomes a felony.

Byte 4 · His words

Two apologies

Vick had his talking points written on a note card for the August 2007 press conference. The Humane Society later auctioned the card. The group pointed out that the biggest apology was the one he never gave, to the dogs. Two years later, out of prison, he sat down with James Brown of CBS.

August 27, 2007 · Richmond · after the guilty plea
"I want to apologize to all the young kids out there for my immature acts."
"Dogfighting is a terrible thing, and I did reject it."
"I will redeem myself. I have to."

He read a statement and took no questions.

August 2009 · 60 Minutes · out of prison
"I should have took the initiative to stop it all. I didn't. I didn't step up."
"I deserved to lose the $130 million."

He answered questions for more than an hour. A lawyer sat in on the interview.

Years after his career ended, Vick sat down with former NFL tight end Shannon Sharpe on the Club Shay Shay podcast. He explained why he kept paying for the operation after he was rich and famous. Watch his answer and listen for whether he is explaining or excusing.

Byte 5 · The money

The swoosh comes back

Nike signed Vick as a rookie in 2001 and ended his contract in August 2007 after he admitted his role. After prison Nike gave him free gear but no paycheck. Then he had the best season of his life.

August 2007DroppedNike ends his deal the week he admits his role.

July 1, 2011Signed againFive months after he wins Comeback Player of the Year.

CNBC reported it was believed to be the first time a brand had ever come back to an athlete it dropped. Nike's statement said the company did not condone what he did but supported the positive changes he had made.

"If you can ball and if you win, we will forgive you."

Job July 27, 2009 · Roger GoodellLet Vick back into the NFL one week after his home confinement ended

He had served his federal sentence. He could sign with any team and play by Week 3.

Job August 13, 2009 · Philadelphia EaglesSign Vick to a one year, $1.6 million contract

None of the money was guaranteed. PETA protested. The owner called it a lot of soul searching.

Honor February 2011 · Associated Press votersName Vick the NFL's Comeback Player of the Year

The award usually goes to a player coming back from an injury. Vick was coming back from prison.

Job July 1, 2011 · NikePay Vick to endorse its products again

Nike had dropped him four years earlier. He had just finished his best NFL season.

Job December 20, 2024 · Norfolk State UniversityHire Vick as head football coach

College coaches recruit and mentor players who are 17 to 22 years old. He had never been a head coach.

Honor December 9, 2025 · National Football FoundationInduct Vick into the College Football Hall of Fame

The honor is for what he did at Virginia Tech in 1999 and 2000, years before the case.
