U.S. History 9

Power & the Gilded Age

The Constitution does not tell the country how to solve every problem. It creates a structure for deciding who has power, how laws are made, how power is limited, and how the system can change.

Preamble

States broad goals for the national government, including justice, domestic tranquility, defense, general welfare, and liberty.

Article I

Creates Congress and gives it legislative powers. Congress may regulate interstate and foreign commerce.

Articles II & III

Article II creates the presidency and executive power. Article III establishes the federal judicial branch.

Article V

Creates a formal process for amending the Constitution when the country decides the basic rules need to change.

10th Amendment

Powers not delegated to the United States, and not prohibited to the states, are reserved to the states or the people.

14th Amendment

Places important limits on state government, including due process and equal protection requirements.

Hidden History

What Does the Document Prove?

Hidden History rule for today: a primary source can prove some things, weaken other claims, and leave many questions unanswered. Your job is to separate those categories.

Articles I–III

The document divides federal authority among Congress, the president, and the federal courts.

Article V

The Constitution can be changed through a formal amendment process.

Article VI

The Constitution and valid federal laws are the supreme law of the land; public officials are bound by oath to support the Constitution.

Bill of Rights

The first ten amendments add protections involving expression, religion, criminal procedure, searches, due process, and other limits on government.

Evidence rule

If the Constitution is silent on a claim, silence does not automatically prove the claim true or false. It may simply be outside the document's subject.

Source check

Beyond the Scoreboard

Who Makes the Rules?

This week, boxing became a modern sport because rules, officials, media, promoters, and government created institutions around the fight. Today, compare that to the rulebook for government itself.

Articles I–III

The Constitution separates lawmaking, execution of laws, and judicial power into different branches.

Commerce power

Article I gives Congress authority over interstate and foreign commerce, which matters when economic activity crosses state lines.

10th Amendment

Powers not assigned to the federal government are generally reserved to the states or the people.

1st Amendment

Limits government interference with speech, press, religion, assembly, and petition.

Article V

The rules can be formally amended instead of requiring the whole system to be discarded whenever change is needed.

Sports connection

A rulebook matters only when people know the rules, officials enforce them, institutions accept them, and there is a process for resolving disputes or changing rules.
