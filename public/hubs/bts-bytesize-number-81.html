Number 81

Aaron Hernandez went from the 113th pick to a $40 million contract to a life sentence for murder. Who saw it coming, and does what doctors found in his brain change the story?

Sit in the 2010 draft room, follow the jerseys fans gave back, read the court record as it changed three times, and look at the scan for yourself.

1

The player

Hernandez grew up in Bristol, Connecticut, the town where ESPN is headquartered. His father Dennis, a former UConn player, died after a routine hernia surgery in January 2006, when Aaron was 16. A year later Aaron left high school early to enroll at the University of Florida.

2009

National champion

Florida beats Oklahoma for the BCS title in January. In December he wins the Mackey Award as the best tight end in college football.

2010

Pick 113

Projected as a first or second rounder, he falls to the fourth round. New England takes him the day after drafting Rob Gronkowski.

2012

Super Bowl touchdown

He scores in Super Bowl XLVI against the Giants in February. In August he signs a five year extension worth about $40 million.

2013

Arrested

On June 26 police lead him from his North Attleborough home in handcuffs. The Patriots release him the same day.

2015

Life sentence

On April 15 a jury convicts him of the first degree murder of Odin Lloyd. He is 25.

    Odin Lloyd

    Odin Lloyd

    1985 to 2013 · Linebacker, Boston Bandits

    Odin Lloyd was 27. He was born in St. Croix, grew up in the Dorchester neighborhood of Boston, worked for a landscaping company and played linebacker for the Boston Bandits, a semi-pro team. He was dating Shaneah Jenkins, the sister of Hernandez's fiancée.

    Most of what has been written about this case is about the famous man who killed him. His mother, Ursula Ward, sat through the whole trial. After the verdict she told the court she forgave the people involved in her son's death.

    1. June 17, 2013

      Lloyd is shot to death in an industrial park in North Attleborough, Massachusetts, about a mile from Hernandez's house. A jogger finds his body that evening.

    2. June 26, 2013

      Hernandez is arrested and charged with murder. About 90 minutes later the Patriots announce they have released him.

    3. Jan to Apr 2015

      The trial in Fall River runs more than two months with over 130 witnesses. Prosecutors never prove a motive.

    4. April 15, 2015

      After about 36 hours of deliberation, the jury finds Hernandez guilty of first degree murder. The sentence is life in prison without parole.

    The brain

    His family donated his brain to Boston University's CTE Center. Chronic traumatic encephalopathy is a disease linked to repeated hits to the head. Doctors can only confirm it after death. Hernandez had played tackle football since he was a kid.
