📈 Market Shock Arena

You run a fictional food truck for eight rounds. Read the market news, set your price and choose your inventory. Unsold stock spoils. Selling out means missed sales. If you cannot fund the next round, the seagulls win.

How this classroom model works

These are invented prices and rules for comparing choices, not real business forecasts. There are no wages, rent, taxes or loans. Every round starts with a $4 ingredient cost per meal, except the shortage round at $6. You pay for every meal you prep, including leftovers.

Expected customers = 22 × the round's demand multiplier × (1 + 0.1 × reputation above or below 3) × your price factor. Price factors: $6 → 1.35, $9 → 1, $13 → 0.62. A random multiplier from 0.9 up to 1.1 adds variation; customers are rounded to a whole number. When the $7 rival arrives, pricing above $7 multiplies your price factor by 0.55.

Demand multipliers, in round order: 1, 1.9, 1, 0.8, 0.5, 1.6, 0.75 and 2.2. Reputation starts at 3, gains 1 after the viral-review round and loses 1 whenever more than 8 customers cannot buy a meal; it stays between 1 and 5. Compare revenue with total prep cost before deciding what to change.
