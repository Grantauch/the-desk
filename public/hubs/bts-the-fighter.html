THE CITY BEHIND THE FIGHTER

Made in Lowell.

Before you decide why Micky keeps fighting, look at where he lives and how he earns a living.

A fighter still has to pay the bills.

Lowell grew around textile mills that turned cotton into cloth. Those jobs drew workers and immigrant families. Then companies closed mills or moved production, leaving the city to rebuild its economy.

The movie opens decades later, in 1993. Micky works paving roads as well as boxing. A fight offers a payday, but earning it puts his body and future at risk.

IN THE FILM

The camera follows Dicky.

The documentary becomes a turning point in his story.

IN THE RECORD

The documentary was real.

High on Crack Street: Lost Lives in Lowell aired in 1995. It followed three people, including Dicky. The movie recreates and reframes material from it.

02
IN THE FILM

A quick path to the title shot.

The story links the Sanchez victory to the opportunity against Neary.

IN THE RECORD

Nearly three years and six more fights.

Ward beat Alfonso Sanchez in April 1997. He fought six more times before facing Shea Neary in March 2000.

1997SANCHEZ→6 MORE BOUTS→2000NEARY

03
IN THE FILM

The title fight ends the main story.

The final bout gives the film its big finish.

IN THE RECORD

London. March 11, 2000. Round eight.

Ward stopped Neary in the eighth round to win the WBU light welterweight title. This was a WBU title, not the undisputed championship.

BoxRec fight record ↗

2002–03

THE QUESTION

Should Micky keep Dicky in his corner?

Write 4–6 sentences. Explain whether Dicky should have a role and what, if any, conditions Micky should set. Use two scenes from the film and consider the strongest argument against your position.

Need help getting started?

Decide what matters most: Dicky's knowledge, his reliability, or Micky's control. Find a scene that supports your choice and another that makes your choice harder. Explain both.
